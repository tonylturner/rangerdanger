European electricity cybersecurity obligations are not a single “substation firewall law.” NIS2 and the electricity Network Code on Cybersecurity (NCCS) have different scopes, mechanisms and reporting clocks. This article explains their relevance to the exercises, not legal advice or a compliance claim.

The source review was conducted on 15 October 2026 for the 8 November 2026 course. No Member State was specified. National implementation, designation notices and NCCS approval dates must be checked before applying any deadline to a real organization.

### NIS2: governance and proportionate risk management

[Directive (EU) 2022/2555](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2555/oj) lists electricity distribution system operators in Annex I. Scope normally includes medium-sized enterprises and larger, with designation and other exceptions. Larger Annex I entities are generally essential; others may be important. “Every DSO is automatically essential” is not a safe summary.

Articles 20–21 cover management approval, oversight and training, and proportionate all-hazards cybersecurity risk management. Topics include incidents, continuity, supply chains, vulnerability handling, control effectiveness, access and assets, cryptography and appropriate multifactor authentication.

Segmentation can support those aims, but Article 21 does not prescribe this range's subnets, historian architecture or TCP/2404 rule. The useful lab output is a justified risk treatment with evidence and residual limitations.

For significant incidents, Article 23 provides warning within 24 hours and notification within 72 hours of awareness, without undue delay, followed by a final report within one month after notification, with arrangements for ongoing incidents. [Commission guidance](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AC%3A2023%3A328%3AFULL) corroborates governance and reporting. National transposition was due 17 October 2024, with application from 18 October; country-specific duties for late 2026 were not verified here.

### NCCS: cross-border electricity impact

[Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1366](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2024/1366/2025-09-14/eng) concerns cybersecurity aspects of cross-border electricity flows. Relevant entity duties depend on high- or critical-impact designation, not simply on being a DSO. Risk management includes assessment, treatment and acceptance, repeated every three years.

Minimum controls apply to high-impact perimeters and advanced controls to critical-impact perimeters. The relevant clocks include controls approval plus 12 months, designation notification plus 12 months for entity risk reporting and plus 24 months for a management system. Designation notification has a latest date of 13 June 2028.

Those relative clocks do not establish a blanket November 2026 compliance deadline. Actual approvals, submissions, notifications and final-control content were not verified. Article 38 has a separate four-hour reportable-attack clock; its late-2026 applicability was not established in this research.

### Separate guidance, planning and law

[ENTSO-E's NCCS material](https://www.entsoe.eu/network_codes/nccs/) distinguishes transitional arrangements and provisional guidance. A provisional list is non-binding guidance, not proof of final control approval. A work programme also does not prove planned deliverables are complete.

[ACER's January 2026 announcement](https://acer.europa.eu/news/acer-launches-guidance-track-cybersecurity-performance-eu-electricity-networks) describes voluntary cybersecurity statistics, with a first 2027 window covering 2026 data. Do not confuse that exercise with mandatory incident notification.

Direct EUR-Lex access was limited during research; indexed legal provisions and official explanatory sources supported the review. Recheck authoritative current text and national advice before operational use. The [risk record](#eu-iec62443-risk-design) and [handover package](#eu-acceptance-handover) illustrate evidence discipline, not proof of NIS2 or NCCS conformity.

Before the course, identify the applicable country, competent authority and entity classification; verify any designation notice and relevant approval dates; and confirm the current reporting procedure. Preserve uncertainty where those facts remain unavailable. A relative deadline cannot be converted into a calendar date by substituting the workshop date for the legal triggering event.
